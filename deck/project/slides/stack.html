4 · TECHNOLOGY CHOICES

Technology options and trade-offs

LayerOptions consideredRecommendation and trade-off
HostingBank’s cloud (in-region) or on-premises GPUsBank’s approved cloud with private networking. On-premises gives more control but is slower and costlier to start.
LLMsHosted frontier models or self-hosted open-weightsHosted for generation; test open-weights for routing and extraction. Quality versus unit cost and control.
OrchestrationLangGraph, Semantic Kernel, or thin custom codeA graph-based framework or thin custom layer. Faster start versus lock-in and harder debugging.
RetrievalAzure AI Search, OpenSearch, pgvectorWhichever the bank already runs, if it supports hybrid search. Fewer new platforms versus niche features.
ObservabilityLangfuse, Arize Phoenix, OpenTelemetryOpenTelemetry traces plus a self-hosted eval tool, so prompts and outputs stay inside the bank.

Final choices are confirmed in discovery against the bank’s cloud strategy, data-residency rules and procurement.

The guiding principle is to reuse what the bank already operates and to avoid lock-in where the market is moving fastest. Hosting follows the bank’s approved cloud and residency rules. For models, the LLM gateway lets us mix hosted frontier models with open-weights models and switch based on evaluation results, not vendor claims. For orchestration, a light graph-based framework or a thin custom layer both work; what matters is that every step is traceable. For retrieval, if the bank already runs a search platform that supports hybrid search, use it. For observability, keep traces and prompts inside the bank: they contain customer data. I have kept vendor names as options, not commitments, because the right answer depends on the bank’s existing estate.
