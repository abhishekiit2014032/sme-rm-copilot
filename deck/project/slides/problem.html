Hypothesis: up to half of an RM’s week is not spent with customers

Illustrative split of an SME RM’s working week

Customer meetings and selling

35%

Meeting prep and data gathering

20%

CRM updates and follow-ups

18%

Credit and onboarding paperwork

17%

Internal policy and product queries

10%

Amber = the 48% the copilot targets directly. Figures are illustrative; they will be replaced by a time-and-motion study in discovery.

Typical pain points

Customer data is spread across CRM, core banking, trade finance, loan origination and document stores
Policy and product questions wait hours or days for credit and product teams
Call reports are logged late or not at all, so pipeline and risk visibility suffer
Follow-ups slip, and cross-sell signals in transaction data go unnoticed

Frame this as a hypothesis, not a finding. Industry commentary consistently says relationship managers spend a large share of time on non-selling work, but the bank’s own split must be measured; that is the first deliverable of discovery. The amber bars are the three activities the copilot targets: meeting prep, CRM and follow-up admin, and internal policy queries, about 48% in this illustration. The right-hand side lists the root causes behind those bars. Each maps to a use case on the next slide: scattered data leads to the pre-meeting brief, slow answers to the policy copilot, and late call reports and missed follow-ups to the follow-up agent.
