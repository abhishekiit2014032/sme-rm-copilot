4 · HANDLING HALLUCINATION

Five layers against wrong answers

1 · GROUND

Answers come only from retrieved passages and tool output. No answering from memory on policy.

2 · CONSTRAIN

Numbers, dates and names are copied from tool output, never generated. Structured output schemas.

3 · VERIFY

Claim-by-claim groundedness check. Figures matched against tool results. Citations must resolve.

4 · SHOW

A source on every line, an “AI-generated” label, and an honest “I can’t confirm this”.

5 · APPROVE

The RM approves every write. Their edits feed the evaluation set.

IF A CHECK FAILS

Regenerate once with stricter instructions if time allows. Otherwise decline, show the sources found and offer a hand-off to the credit or product desk.

Hallucination cannot be eliminated by prompting alone, so we defend in depth. Ground: the model answers only from what retrieval and tools return. Constrain: anything factual and numeric, such as a balance, a limit or a date, is inserted from tool output rather than generated, and outputs follow a schema. Verify: a separate check breaks the answer into claims and confirms each is supported by its cited source; numbers are matched exactly against tool results. Show: the RM sees the source for every line, so they can check it in a click. Approve: nothing is written without the RM. When a check fails, the system prefers a safe decline over a confident guess. The three numbers at the bottom are design rules and release criteria, not measured results.
